# Research Papers and References

This collection contains research papers relevant to the taxonomy, detection,
evaluation, and mitigation of hallucinations in Large Language Models (LLMs),
with particular emphasis on research and scholarly applications.

The papers are organized according to their relevance to the taxonomy developed
in the accompanying research paper.

---

## 1. Hallucination Surveys and Taxonomies

### 1. A Survey on Hallucination in Large Language Models: Principles, Taxonomy, Challenges, and Open Questions

**Authors:** Lei Huang et al.  
**Year:** 2025  
**Venue:** ACM Transactions on Information Systems  
**DOI:** 10.1145/3703155  
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3703155

A comprehensive survey of hallucinations in LLMs. It distinguishes factuality
and faithfulness hallucinations and examines their causes, detection methods,
benchmarks, and mitigation strategies.

**Relevance:** Provides an important foundation for the taxonomy used in this
research.

---

### 2. Survey of Hallucination in Natural Language Generation

**Authors:** Ziwei Ji et al.  
**Year:** 2023  
**Venue:** ACM Computing Surveys  
**DOI:** 10.1145/3571730  
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3571730

A major survey of hallucination in natural language generation. It discusses
the distinction between factuality and faithfulness hallucination.

**Relevance:** Forms the conceptual basis for the factuality/faithfulness axis.

---

### 3. A Survey of Hallucination in Large Foundation Models

**Authors:** V. Rawte, A. Sheth, A. Das  
**Year:** 2023  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05922

Reviews hallucination phenomena in large foundation models and proposes
different ways of organizing hallucination types.

**Relevance:** Contributes to the taxonomy comparison discussed in the paper.

---

### 4. Cognitive Mirage: A Review of Hallucinations in Large Language Models

**Authors:** H. Ye, T. Liu, A. Zhang, W. Hua, W. Jia  
**Year:** 2023  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06794

Reviews hallucinations related to reasoning and multi-step generation.

**Relevance:** Particularly relevant to logical and reasoning hallucinations.

---

### 5. Siren's Song in the AI Ocean: A Survey on Hallucination in Large Language Models

**Authors:** Y. Zhang et al.  
**Year:** 2023  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01219

Surveys hallucination mechanisms and distinguishes several forms of
input-, context-, and fact-conflicting hallucination.

**Relevance:** Helps explain why different taxonomies use overlapping but
different terminology.

## 2. Foundational Work on Factuality and Faithfulness

### 6. On Faithfulness and Factuality in Abstractive Summarization

**Authors:** Joshua Maynez, Shashi Narayan, Bernd Bohnet, Ryan McDonald  
**Year:** 2020  
**Venue:** ACL  
**DOI:** 10.18653/v1/2020.acl-main.173  
**Link:** https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.173/

Introduces the influential distinction between intrinsic and extrinsic
hallucinations in abstractive summarization.

**Relevance:** Provides the foundation for the origin-based axis of this
research taxonomy.

---

### 7. Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks

**Authors:** Patrick Lewis et al.  
**Year:** 2020  
**Venue:** NeurIPS  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401

Introduces Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), which combines generation
with retrieval from an external knowledge source.

**Relevance:** Important for understanding hallucination in retrieval-grounded
research applications.

## 4. Hallucination Detection and Evaluation

### 10. TruthfulQA: Measuring How Models Mimic Human Falsehoods

**Authors:** Stephanie Lin, Jacob Hilton, Owain Evans  
**Year:** 2022  
**Venue:** ACL  
**DOI:** 10.18653/v1/2022.acl-long.229  
**Link:** https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.229/

**Relevance:** Provides a benchmark-based approach to factuality evaluation.

### 11. HaluEval: A Large-Scale Hallucination Evaluation Benchmark for Large Language Models

**Authors:** Junyi Li, Xiaozhou Cheng, Wayne Xin Zhao, J. Y. Nie, Ji-Rong Wen  
**Year:** 2023  
**Venue:** EMNLP  
**Link:** https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.397/

Provides a large-scale benchmark containing hallucinated and non-hallucinated
responses.

**Relevance:** Useful for evaluating hallucination detection systems.

---

### 12. FActScore: Fine-grained Atomic Evaluation of Factual Precision in Long Form Text Generation

**Authors:** Sewon Min et al.  
**Year:** 2023  
**Venue:** EMNLP  
**Link:** https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.741/

Evaluates long-form generated text by decomposing it into atomic factual
claims and checking their support.

**Relevance:** Particularly useful for research writing and long-form
literature synthesis.

---

### 13. SelfCheckGPT: Zero-Resource Black-Box Hallucination Detection for Generative Large Language Models

**Authors:** Potsawee Manakul, Adian Liusie, Mark J. F. Gales  
**Year:** 2023  
**Venue:** EMNLP  
**Link:** https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.557/

Uses consistency across multiple sampled responses to identify potentially
hallucinated information without requiring model internals.

**Relevance:** Represents sampling-based hallucination detection.

---

### 14. Detecting Hallucinations in Large Language Models Using Semantic Entropy

**Authors:** Sebastian Farquhar, Jannik Kossen, Lorenz Kuhn, Yarin Gal  
**Year:** 2024  
**Venue:** Nature  
**DOI:** 10.1038/s41586-024-07421-0

**Relevance:** Represents uncertainty-based hallucination detection.

## How These Papers Relate to the Proposed Taxonomy

The research paper organizes hallucinations along four complementary axes:

1. **Origin**
   - Intrinsic
   - Extrinsic

2. **Evaluative Target**
   - Factuality
   - Faithfulness

3. **Content Type**
   - Entity and attribute
   - Citation and reference
   - Numerical and statistical
   - Methodological and procedural
   - Logical and reasoning
   - Source attribution

4. **Task Context**
   - Closed-book generation
   - Retrieval-Augmented Generation
   - Long-form and multi-step synthesis

A single hallucinated output can belong to multiple categories across these
axes. This multi-axis structure is intended to make hallucination analysis
more useful for research and scholarly-writing applications.
